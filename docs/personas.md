# Flex Platform — User Personas

Key personas across Flex's customer, partner, and internal audiences. Used as a primary input for product, design, and content decisions. Each persona covers context, goals, pain points, platform relationship, key questions, and engagement profile.

---

## How to use this document

These personas are not exhaustive user research — they are a shared working model to align decisions across product, design, engineering, and commercial. Where a feature or piece of content creates tension between personas, use this document to name the trade-off explicitly rather than defaulting to one audience unconsciously.

Personas are grouped into three categories:
- **Customers** — end users with energy assets under Blackcurrent management
- **Market & Industry** — external stakeholders who interact with Flex data or assets indirectly
- **Internal** — Blackcurrent team members who use or build the platform

## Customer personas

---

### 1. Commercial Property Owner

**Representative name:** Greg, 54 — property developer and landlord, Hamilton

**One-line summary:** Bought solar and battery as a business investment. Wants to see it paying off without having to manage it.

**Context.** Greg owns a mix of commercial properties — a light industrial unit in Te Rapa, two retail tenancies in Rototuna, and a small office building. He went solar on the office building after a tenant pushed for it and energy costs kept rising. He signed a Blackcurrent EaaS agreement and sees the system as infrastructure, not a project. He is not deeply interested in energy; he is interested in his bottom line.

**Goals on Flex.**
- Confirm the system is working without having to ask anyone
- See the dollar impact: what is the system saving him, and is that in line with what he was sold
- Have something credible to show tenants and accountants when they ask about the system

**Pain points.**
- Has no time for platforms that require learning or effort
- Distrusts dashboards that show data without explaining what it means
- Feels out of his depth if a technician uses energy jargon
- Has been burned before by technology promises that didn't translate to savings

**Key questions.**
- "Is this thing actually working?"
- "Am I saving what I was told I'd save?"
- "What do I do if something goes wrong — who do I call?"

**Tech fluency.** Low–Medium. Comfortable with email and smartphones. Unlikely to explore beyond what is immediately in front of him.

**Engagement pattern.** Occasional — checks in when prompted (a report, an alert, a tenant question). Would not log in more than once a month unless there was a problem.

### 2. Engaged Dairy Farm Owner

**Representative name:** Blair, 44 — dairy farmer, Waikato

**One-line summary:** High-energy-cost operation, hands-on personality, genuinely wants to understand what his system is doing and push it harder.

**Context.** Blair runs a 500-cow dairy operation. His electricity bill is one of his top three operating costs. He invested in a large PV array and battery through Flex Energy (Farmlands Flex channel) and approached it the same way he approaches any capital investment — he wants to understand it well enough to make better decisions. He reads the data, asks his Blackcurrent contact questions, and compares notes with other farmers in his area.

**Goals on Flex.**
- Understand energy flows at a level that lets him make decisions (milking shed timing, irrigation scheduling, EV charger management)
- Know whether the system is performing as well as it should
- Use data to have informed conversations with his retailer about tariffs
- Eventually participate in demand response or ancillary markets if it makes commercial sense

**Pain points.**
- Gets frustrated when data is presented without enough context to act on it
- Wants to see actual vs expected performance, not just raw kWh
- Connectivity on the farm can be patchy — sometimes data gaps make him question whether the system is working
- Doesn't want a dumbed-down view; he can handle complexity if it's presented clearly

**Key questions.**
- "Is my battery charging at the right time or am I leaving money on the table?"
- "How does my self-consumption this month compare to last month and to what you promised?"
- "When the grid goes down, does my system actually island properly?"

**Tech fluency.** Medium–High. Comfortable with digital tools, uses farm management software, not intimidated by data.

**Engagement pattern.** Regular — checks in daily or several times a week during active periods. Would engage with alerts, schedule changes, and performance reports.

### 3. Disengaged Dairy Farm Owner

**Representative name:** Kevin, 61 — dairy farmer, Southland

**One-line summary:** Bought the system on the recommendation of a trusted advisor. Wants it to work in the background and never need to think about it.

**Context.** Kevin agreed to solar and battery because his Farmlands rep convinced him the economics stacked up and his neighbour had done it. He signed the paperwork, the system was installed, and he considers it done. He has no interest in dashboards, data, or energy management theory. If the power bill goes down and nothing breaks, he is satisfied. He will engage only if something goes wrong or someone makes him.

**Goals on Flex.**
- Not to need it
- If he does need it, to get an answer in under 30 seconds with no jargon

**Pain points.**
- Any platform that requires effort, explanation, or training is already a failure from his perspective
- Alert fatigue — if he gets notifications about things that turn out to be nothing, he will ignore all future alerts
- Feels patronised by energy content written for enthusiasts
- Has low trust in technology "optimising" things without his oversight

**Key questions.**
- "Is there a problem I need to worry about?"
- "What do I need to do, if anything?"

**Tech fluency.** Low. Uses a smartphone for calls and messaging. Would not naturally navigate a web dashboard.

**Engagement pattern.** Rare — reacts only to alerts or direct outreach. Would rely on Blackcurrent operations or a farm advisor to interpret any data on his behalf.

### 4. Dairy Farmer's Wife

**Representative name:** Helen, 49 — farm co-owner and administrator, Waikato

**One-line summary:** Manages the farm's finances and administration. Sees energy costs and the system's performance as her domain, not her husband's.

**Context.** Helen handles accounts payable, the farm budget, GST returns, and supplier relationships. She was in the room when Blackcurrent presented the business case and she remembers the numbers better than Blair does. She is the one who receives and queries the power bills and would notice immediately if savings weren't materialising. She is not an energy expert but she is commercially sharp and organised.

**Goals on Flex.**
- Monthly or periodic reporting she can reconcile against the power bill
- Evidence that the system is delivering the savings promised in the original business case
- Clean records she can hand to an accountant or show during a farm valuation

**Pain points.**
- Frustrated when reported savings don't obviously correspond to the actual power bill (reconciliation gap)
- Wants exportable data (CSV, PDF) not just a dashboard she has to screenshot
- Does not want to log into a separate system for every task — prefers reports delivered to her

**Key questions.**
- "What did we save this month in dollar terms?"
- "Is the system delivering what the business case said it would?"
- "Can I get a summary I can attach to the monthly accounts?"

**Tech fluency.** Medium. Competent with email, spreadsheets, Xero. Would use a web platform if it was well-designed but expects it to work first time.

**Engagement pattern.** Monthly — tied to billing cycles. Would engage with scheduled reports more than ad-hoc logins.

### 5. School Principal

**Representative name:** Diane, 52 — principal, contributing school, Auckland

**One-line summary:** Made the solar and battery decision for sustainability reasons and cost relief. Now carries the responsibility of explaining it to the board and the community.

**Context.** Diane pushed for the solar system as part of a broader sustainability initiative and because the school's electricity costs had become a material line in the annual budget. She worked with the board and the Ministry to get it approved and is proud of the decision. She now fields questions from the board, parents, and occasionally media about what the system is doing. She does not want to be caught out not knowing her own system's performance.

**Goals on Flex.**
- Be able to answer board questions about cost savings and sustainability impact confidently
- Share visible evidence of the system's performance with the school community (newsletters, assemblies, parent evenings)
- Know quickly if something is wrong so she can get it resolved without disruption to the school day

**Pain points.**
- Has no energy background — needs everything translated into plain language and dollars
- The system needs to work quietly; visible failures damage trust with parents and the board
- Reporting cycles are annual (budget, board reports) — she needs structured summaries, not daily data
- Does not have the time or headspace to learn a complex platform

**Key questions.**
- "How much have we saved this year?"
- "How much CO₂ have we avoided — can I put that in the newsletter?"
- "Is the system working properly right now?"

**Tech fluency.** Medium. Comfortable with digital tools in her administrative role. Would use a well-designed dashboard but only if it immediately gave her what she needed.

**Engagement pattern.** Periodic — driven by reporting deadlines, board meetings, and community events. Would strongly prefer automated reports over manual logins.

### 6. Transpower System Operator

**Representative name:** Anaru, 38 — system operations analyst, Transpower Wellington

**One-line summary:** Responsible for real-time and near-term grid stability. Cares about what distributed assets are available, dispatchable, and reliable at the portfolio level.

**Context.** Anaru works within Transpower's system operations function, which is responsible for keeping the New Zealand grid in balance in real time. He is aware of Blackcurrent through initiatives like FlexPoint (Transpower's demand flexibility programme) and sees distributed energy resources as an increasingly important part of the frequency and reserve toolkit. He thinks in megawatts, availability windows, and dispatch response times — not in customer savings.

**Goals on Flex.**
- Understand the aggregate flexible capacity available from Blackcurrent's portfolio at any given time
- Confirm assets can respond within required timeframes for any demand response or ancillary service obligations
- Have confidence that dispatch signals will be honoured and metered accurately

**Pain points.**
- Distributed energy assets have historically been opaque — hard to know what's available until you try to dispatch
- Coordination with multiple parties (retailer, aggregator, platform) adds latency and complexity
- Data quality and reporting standards from DER aggregators are inconsistent

**Key questions.**
- "What capacity is available in the next 30 minutes?"
- "What is the response time and reliability track record of this portfolio?"
- "How is dispatch performance reported and audited?"

**Tech fluency.** High. Works with complex operational systems daily.

**Engagement pattern.** Periodic and event-driven — would interact primarily through APIs, dispatch interfaces, and formal reporting rather than a consumer-facing dashboard.

### 7. Lines Company Network Planner

**Representative name:** Sarah, 45 — network planning manager, regional lines company

**One-line summary:** Responsible for the long-term capacity and reliability of the distribution network. Wants to understand what DER assets are on her network and whether they're a help or a hindrance.

**Context.** Sarah manages capital planning for a regional lines company. Distributed solar and batteries are changing her load profiles in ways that are harder to predict than traditional load growth. She needs to understand where assets are, how they behave across the day, and whether they will reduce or worsen peak demand on constrained parts of the network. She is also watching emerging managed network services (MNS) schemes that could allow her to pay DER aggregators to manage network constraints.

**Goals on Flex.**
- Portfolio-level visibility into where assets are and how they behave
- Understanding of how assets respond during peak periods — are they helping or exporting at the wrong time?
- A pathway to coordinated demand management on constrained feeders

**Pain points.**
- Currently has very little visibility into behind-the-meter assets — they appear only as unusual load profiles at the zone substation level
- Regulatory and technical coordination with aggregators is still immature in NZ
- Worried about solar export at midday causing overvoltage on rural feeders

**Key questions.**
- "Which feeders have significant DER exposure and how does that affect my peak demand assumptions?"
- "If I need to curtail export on a feeder during a constraint event, can you help and how quickly?"
- "What does your portfolio look like and how is it growing?"

**Tech fluency.** High — uses GIS, load flow modelling, and operational systems routinely.

**Engagement pattern.** Infrequent — strategic conversations, data sharing, and formal reports rather than regular platform access.

### 8. Blackcurrent Operations Team

**Representative name:** Mia, 31 — energy operations analyst, Blackcurrent

**One-line summary:** Monitors the live fleet, triages alerts, coordinates with field teams, and is the first internal response when something on a customer site goes wrong.

**Context.** Mia looks at the Flex platform for a large portion of her working day. She is responsible for catching issues before customers notice them, coordinating maintenance and commissioning, and making sure the data Blackcurrent relies on is clean and accurate. She knows the platform better than anyone and pushes hardest for operational workflow improvements.

**Goals on Flex.**
- Fast, reliable identification of sites with problems — without having to check each one individually
- Clear triage prioritisation: which sites are critical, which can wait
- Efficient tools for investigating a site in depth when something looks wrong
- A record of actions taken for accountability and continuity

**Pain points.**
- Alert fatigue when too many low-severity events require acknowledgement
- Having to jump between multiple tools (platform, spreadsheets, email threads) to complete a workflow
- Data gaps or telemetry outages that make it hard to distinguish a real problem from a connectivity issue
- Customer-facing information lagging behind what she can see internally

**Key questions.**
- "Which sites need attention right now?"
- "Is this a real fault or a data gap?"
- "What was the last action taken on this site and by whom?"

**Tech fluency.** High — a power user. Expects the platform to keep up with her, not the other way around.

**Engagement pattern.** Daily, sustained — one of the most frequent and demanding platform users.

### 9. Product Manager

**Representative name:** Paddy, 35 — product manager, Blackcurrent

**One-line summary:** Responsible for the direction and prioritisation of the Flex platform. Needs to synthesise customer needs, technical constraints, and commercial goals into a coherent roadmap.

**Context.** Paddy works across customer research, engineering, design, and commercial to decide what Flex should do next and why. He uses the platform himself to understand it as a user would, reads PRDs and personas like this one as thinking inputs, and is accountable for outcomes — not just shipped features. He is most effective when he has clear signal about what is working, what is not, and what users are actually asking for.

**Goals on Flex.**
- Clear visibility into which features are being used and which are not
- Fast feedback loops between prototypes, real users, and the development team
- A platform that is coherent — each module should feel like it belongs to the same product
- Evidence that the platform is creating real customer value, not just being used

**Pain points.**
- Competing priorities from commercial, operations, and engineering without a shared framework for trade-offs
- Gaps between what users say they want and what they actually do
- The risk of building for internal power users (operations) at the expense of external customers
- Maintaining a high-quality prototype that stakeholders can use to test ideas before committing to engineering effort

**Key questions.**
- "Which personas are we designing for in this release?"
- "What does the data tell us about how this feature is being used?"
- "What is the most important problem we are not solving?"

**Tech fluency.** High. Comfortable reading and writing technical specifications, reviewing code, and building prototypes.

**Engagement pattern.** Daily — uses the platform as both a product being built and a tool for doing his job.

### 10. Software Developer

**Representative name:** Leo, 28 — full-stack developer, Blackcurrent

**One-line summary:** Builds and maintains Flex. Needs clear requirements, clean data contracts, and enough context about the business problem to make good technical decisions.

**Context.** Leo works across the Flex platform — frontend, backend, and data pipelines. He is effective when he understands not just what to build but why, so he can make sensible trade-offs when the spec runs into reality. He is a key voice on technical feasibility and architecture. He also maintains and extends the prototype HTML platform used for product testing and stakeholder demonstrations.

**Goals on Flex.**
- Requirements that are specific enough to implement without constant clarification
- A clear understanding of the data model — what fields exist, what they mean, where they come from
- Confidence that the architecture today won't create painful constraints tomorrow
- Feedback loops that help him understand whether what he shipped actually worked

**Pain points.**
- Vague requirements that only become clear after something is built and reviewed
- Data quality issues that make it hard to know whether a feature is broken or the underlying data is bad
- Context loss between prototype decisions and production implementation
- Being pulled between competing priorities without a clear tiebreaker

**Key questions.**
- "What does this field actually represent and what is its source?"
- "What are the edge cases I need to handle?"
- "Is this a one-off or something that needs to be a pattern?"

**Tech fluency.** Expert.

**Engagement pattern.** Daily — builds and tests features continuously.

### 11. Investor

**Representative name:** Richard, 58 — private equity / infrastructure investor

**One-line summary:** Has capital committed to or considering commitment to Blackcurrent. Wants to see a scalable business model with defensible technology and a clear path to returns.

**Context.** Richard thinks in portfolio terms. He has evaluated a number of energy transition businesses and knows that the hardware side of solar and battery is commoditised — the value, if it exists, is in the software, customer relationships, and recurring revenue model. He will engage with operational data at a high level but is primarily interested in the commercial narrative: how many customers, how much recurring revenue, what is the retention rate, and how does the platform create defensible competitive advantage.

**Goals on Flex.**
- Confidence that the platform creates genuine customer lock-in and switching costs
- Evidence that the data and software layer is real, not a thin reporting UI on top of commodity hardware
- Visibility into portfolio scale and growth — number of sites, assets under management, revenue per site
- A credible path to market participation (flexibility, aggregation) that justifies the software investment

**Pain points.**
- Energy technology businesses that overstate software capability to attract investment
- Difficulty distinguishing between prototype demonstrations and production-grade systems
- Uncertainty about how NZ market structure evolution (flexibility markets, distributed network services) will affect the business model

**Key questions.**
- "How many sites are live and what is the data coverage?"
- "What does a customer look like three years into the relationship?"
- "What is the moat — why won't a customer just use a competitor's monitoring app?"

**Tech fluency.** Medium. Understands technology at a conceptual level; does not review code.

**Engagement pattern.** Periodic — formal reporting cycles, board presentations, and due diligence events. Would not use the operational platform directly.

### 12. Senior Leadership Team

**Representative name:** The exec team — CEO, CTO, Head of Commercial

**One-line summary:** Setting direction for Blackcurrent. Needs enough operational and strategic visibility to make confident decisions about where to invest and what to commit to customers and partners.

**Context.** The SLT moves between strategic planning, customer relationships, investor reporting, and operational oversight. They rely on the operations team for day-to-day platform performance and on the product manager for roadmap decisions — but they need to be able to form a credible view of portfolio health and platform progress without going into the weeds. They are accountable for the commitments Blackcurrent makes externally.

**Goals on Flex.**
- Reliable, high-level portfolio metrics that can be used in board reports and investor updates
- Early warning of anything that could become a customer relationship or reputational issue
- Confidence that the product roadmap reflects both commercial priorities and what customers actually need
- Evidence that the platform is an asset, not a liability — it should reinforce, not undermine, Blackcurrent's positioning

**Pain points.**
- Getting stuck in operational detail when they need strategic signal
- Platform maturity gaps that create credibility risk when demonstrated to partners or investors
- Difficulty articulating the software value proposition in a way that is specific and differentiated
- Misalignment between what the product team is building and what the commercial team is selling

**Key questions.**
- "What is the overall health of our portfolio right now?"
- "Are our customers getting the value we promised?"
- "What is the product team shipping this quarter and does it matter commercially?"

**Tech fluency.** Variable — CTO is high, CEO and Head of Commercial are medium. All expect polished, well-structured outputs from the platform and team.

**Engagement pattern.** Weekly or monthly summary cadence. Would interact with dashboards and reports rather than operational platform views.

### 13. Board Member

**Representative name:** Jane, 62 — independent board director

**One-line summary:** Governance role. Holds the business accountable for strategy, risk, and fiduciary obligations. Needs enough information to exercise judgement, not enough to become operational.

**Context.** Jane sits on two boards and brings experience from the energy sector. She understands the broad commercial landscape — electricity markets, distributed energy, infrastructure investment — but is not close to the day-to-day operations of Blackcurrent. She relies on management to present information well and escalate issues appropriately. She is most concerned with risk, long-term positioning, and whether the business has the right foundations to grow.

**Goals on Flex.**
- Confidence that the technology asset is real and creating durable customer value
- A clear and honest account of where the business is vs. where it said it would be
- Sight of material risks — customer concentration, data quality, market structure change, competition
- Evidence of a coherent product strategy with appropriate resource behind it

**Pain points.**
- Information presented at the wrong level — too operational to be actionable at board level, or too vague to provide genuine assurance
- Overconfident forward-looking statements without appropriate caveats
- Being surprised by issues that should have been escalated earlier

**Key questions.**
- "Is the product delivering on the promises made to customers and investors?"
- "What are the three biggest risks to the business's technology position?"
- "Where is the business in three years if the current strategy succeeds?"

**Tech fluency.** Low–Medium. Expects high-quality, concise reporting. Does not interact with the platform directly.

**Engagement pattern.** Board meeting cadence — typically quarterly. Receives curated summary packs from management.
